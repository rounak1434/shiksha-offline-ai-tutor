Newton's second law of motion says that **force equals mass times acceleration** (F = ma). 

**Simple example:**
- If a car is moving at 60 km/h and you apply a force to it, it will accelerate.
- If the force is too large, the car will stop or reverse.
- If the force is too small, the car will not accelerate.

**Key points:**
- Force = mass × acceleration
- Mass = mass (doesn't change)
- Acceleration = force / mass
- More force = more acceleration
- More mass = less acceleration

**Formula:**
F = m × a

**Example:**
- A 10 kg car is moving at 20 m/s. What is the force needed to stop it in 10 seconds?
- Acceleration = (Final velocity - Initial velocity) / time
- Final velocity = 0 (stopped)
- Acceleration = (0 - 20) / 10 = -2 m/s²
- Force = mass × acceleration = 10 kg × -2 m/s² = -20 N (force in opposite direction)

**Key takeaway:**
- Force is what makes things move or stop.
- Mass doesn't change, but acceleration depends on force and mass.

**Mass vs Weight: Key Differences**

**Mass** is the amount of matter in an object. It is constant and measured in kilograms (kg).  
**Weight** is the force due to gravity acting on an object. It depends on the gravitational field strength (acceleration due to gravity).

**Example:**
- A 10 kg object on Earth has a weight of 98 N (9.8 m/s² * 10 kg).
- A 10 kg object on the Moon has a weight of 16 N (1.6 m/s² * 10 kg).

**Key point:**
- Mass is constant (10 kg).
- Weight changes with location (98 N on Earth, 16 N on the Moon).

**Formula:**
Weight = Mass × Acceleration due to gravity (g)  
Weight = 10 kg × 9.8 m/s² = 98 N
